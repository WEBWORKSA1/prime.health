Cardio gets the headlines, but muscle is what keeps you climbing stairs, carrying groceries and getting up off the floor at 85. It also acts like a metabolic sponge, soaking up blood sugar after meals. The encouraging part: the dose that delivers most of the benefit is small.

Why muscle matters for a long life

Adults gradually lose muscle from midlife, and the loss accelerates after about 60. When strength and muscle fall far enough, doctors call it sarcopenia, which raises the risk of falls, fractures, hospital stays and loss of independence. Stronger muscles also improve insulin sensitivity, support bone density and protect joints.

How much is enough

A 2022 meta-analysis in the British Journal of Sports Medicine pooled 16 studies and found that 30–60 minutes a week of muscle-strengthening activity was associated with 10–20% lower risk of death from any cause, heart disease and cancer. Combining it with aerobic activity was linked to even lower risk. The World Health Organization recommends muscle-strengthening on two or more days a week for all adults, and balance work for older adults.

Six movement patterns

  • Squat: sit-to-stand from a chair, goblet squat, leg press.
  • Hinge: hip hinge with a dowel, Romanian deadlift, kettlebell deadlift.
  • Push: wall or incline push-up, dumbbell bench press, overhead press.
  • Pull: band row, one-arm dumbbell row, lat pull-down.
  • Carry: farmer's carry with two heavy bags or dumbbells.
  • Brace: plank, side plank, dead bug.

Choose a load that leaves 1–3 good reps in reserve at the end of each set. The whole session takes about 40 minutes.

How to progress

When you can complete the top of the rep range on every set with good form, add a little load (2–5%), a rep or a set. Progress is rarely linear; a stall for a few weeks is normal. Use the one-rep max calculator to set loads once you know your working weights, and re-check every 4–8 weeks.

Fuel and recovery

Muscle is built between sessions. A 2018 meta-analysis found protein intake up to about 1.6 g per kg per day enhanced strength-training gains, with little extra benefit beyond that on average. Spread protein across meals, sleep at least seven hours and keep at least one rest day between sessions for the same muscles.

Safety

Learn technique with light loads, breathe out during the effort, and stop for sharp or joint pain. If you have heart disease, uncontrolled high blood pressure, osteoporosis or a recent injury, ask a clinician or physiotherapist to tailor the plan.
