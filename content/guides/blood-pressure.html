High blood pressure rarely causes symptoms, yet it is the leading modifiable cause of heart attack and stroke worldwide. The good news: few health numbers respond as reliably to everyday changes.

The categories

CategorySystolic (top)Diastolic (bottom)
Normalbelow 120andbelow 80
Elevated120–129andbelow 80
Stage 1 hypertension130–139or80–89
Stage 2 hypertension140 or higheror90 or higher

Readings above 180 and/or 120 are in the severe range and need prompt medical attention, urgently if there are symptoms such as chest pain, breathlessness, weakness or vision changes.

What changed in 2025

The American Heart Association and American College of Cardiology, with 11 partner organisations, kept the 2017 categories. The changes are in what happens next: a treatment target below 130/80 for most adults, the PREVENT risk calculator to decide who needs medicine at stage 1, a preference for starting two medicines in one pill at stage 2, and wider screening for causes such as primary aldosteronism. People with stage 1 and lower risk start with three to six months of lifestyle change.

Measure it right at home

  1. Use a validated upper-arm cuff that fits.
  2. No caffeine, exercise or smoking for 30 minutes; empty your bladder.
  3. Sit with back supported, feet flat, arm resting at heart level, for 5 minutes.
  4. Take two readings a minute apart, morning and evening, for 7 days.
  5. Average them (the checker does this for up to three readings at once).

Effects partly add up. Two or three changes together can match a medicine.

Sodium, simply

Most sodium comes from packaged and restaurant food, not the salt shaker. Bread, cured meats, cheese, soups, sauces and takeaways are the big sources. Compare labels and choose versions with less sodium per serving; cook more meals at home; add flavour with herbs, citrus and spices. If you have kidney disease, ask before using potassium-based salt substitutes.

When medicine is the right call

Medicine is not a failure. At stage 2, or stage 1 with heart disease, diabetes, kidney disease or high calculated risk, guidelines recommend medicine alongside lifestyle change, because lowering pressure cuts strokes and heart attacks substantially. Lifestyle work still matters: it can mean fewer pills and better results.
