Protein supplies the amino acids your body uses to repair muscle, make enzymes and hormones, and support immunity. Most adults eat enough to avoid deficiency. Fewer eat enough, at the right times, to build and keep muscle as they age.

The RDA is a floor

The Recommended Dietary Allowance of 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day was set to prevent deficiency in nearly all healthy adults. For a 75 kg person that is 60 g. It was not designed to maximise strength, recovery or healthy ageing.

Spread it across the day

Muscle building responds to each protein-rich meal. Research suggests about 0.4 g per kg per meal, roughly 25–40 g for most adults, across three or four meals. Many people eat 10 g at breakfast and 60 g at dinner; shifting some to breakfast is one of the easiest upgrades.

Plant-based eaters

Combine legumes, soy, whole grains, nuts and seeds through the day and aim toward the upper end of your range. Soy, pea and mixed plant protein powders are useful top-ups.

When to be careful

People with chronic kidney disease may need to limit protein and should follow their nephrologist or dietitian. Pregnancy and breastfeeding raise needs. Protein shakes are not a replacement for meals in children or teens.
