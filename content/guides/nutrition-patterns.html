Nutrition news swings from one villain to the next, but the long-term evidence keeps pointing the same way. The eating patterns linked to longer, healthier lives look surprisingly alike.

What healthy patterns share

  • Plenty of vegetables, fruit, legumes, nuts and whole grains
  • Mostly unsaturated fats such as olive oil, nuts, seeds and fish
  • Enough protein from a mix of plant and animal sources
  • Few sugary drinks, refined grains and ultra-processed snacks
  • Moderate or no alcohol

The Mediterranean evidence

PREDIMED, a large Spanish trial, randomised adults at high cardiovascular risk to a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts, or to advice on a lower-fat diet. After an initial publication was corrected and re-analysed in 2018, both Mediterranean groups still had about 30% fewer major cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death). Few diet trials of this size show hard outcomes.

Fibre is the most under-eaten nutrient

A 2019 Lancet series of meta-analyses commissioned by the World Health Organization found that people eating 25–29 g of fibre a day had 15–30% lower risk of death from any cause, coronary heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer than those eating the least, with further benefit above that. Most adults eat well under 20 g. The National Academies' target is about 14 g per 1,000 kcal, roughly 25 g for women and 38 g for men.

Easy fibre wins: oats or bran at breakfast, beans or lentils in one meal a day, berries and pears, whole-grain bread, and two vegetables at dinner.

Ultra-processed food

In a tightly controlled NIH trial in 2019, adults lived in a research unit and ate either an ultra-processed or a minimally processed diet for two weeks each, matched for available calories, sugar, fat, fibre and sodium. On the ultra-processed diet they ate about 500 kcal a day more and gained weight; on the minimally processed diet they lost it. Such foods tend to be soft, energy-dense and easy to eat quickly.

Ten swaps that add up

  1. Sugary drink → sparkling water with citrus
  2. White bread → whole-grain or rye
  3. Butter for cooking → olive oil
  4. Chips → a handful of nuts
  5. Processed meat → chicken, fish or beans
  6. Sweetened yogurt → plain yogurt with fruit
  7. Creamy sauces → tomato or herb-based
  8. Refined cereal → oats
  9. Dessert most nights → fruit most nights
  10. Takeaway twice a week → once
