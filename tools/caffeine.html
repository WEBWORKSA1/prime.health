Caffeine calculator

Tally today's caffeine, compare it with safe limits and see roughly how much will still be circulating when you go to bed.

How it works

Totals use typical caffeine contents. Carry-over at bedtime uses a 5-hour half-life, treating all of today's caffeine as if taken at your last drink, so it is an upper estimate:

remaining = total × 0.5^(hours until bed ÷ 5)

Reading your result

The U.S. FDA cites 400 mg a day as an amount not generally linked to negative effects in healthy adults. During pregnancy, obstetric guidance advises staying below 200 mg. If you sleep poorly, try a caffeine cut-off about eight hours before bed.

Limits to keep in mind

Caffeine content varies widely: a large coffee-shop brew can exceed 300 mg. Sensitivity also varies; some medicines, pregnancy and liver conditions slow clearance, while smoking speeds it up.

Questions people ask

How much caffeine is too much?

Above 400 mg a day for most healthy adults, or less if you notice jitters, a racing heart or poor sleep.

How long does caffeine last?

Its half-life averages about 5 hours, so half of an afternoon coffee may still be active at bedtime.

Is decaf caffeine-free?

Nearly. A cup typically has 2–15 mg.
