How it works

Blood pressure has two numbers. Systolic (top) is the pressure when the heart beats; diastolic (bottom) is the pressure between beats. Your category is set by whichever number falls in the higher category.

CategorySystolicDiastolic
Normalbelow 120andbelow 80
Elevated120–129andbelow 80
Stage 1 hypertension130–139or80–89
Stage 2 hypertension140 or higheror90 or higher
Severe rangeabove 180and/orabove 120

The 2025 guideline kept the 2017 categories, set a target below 130/80 for most adults and introduced the PREVENT risk calculator to guide treatment decisions.

Reading your result

How to measure well: sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported and feet flat; rest your arm at heart level on a table; use a cuff that fits your upper arm; no caffeine, exercise or smoking for 30 minutes; take two readings a minute apart and average them. Home readings over a week are more reliable than a single clinic reading.

Elevated and stage 1 readings usually start with lifestyle steps: less sodium, more potassium-rich foods, regular activity, weight loss if needed and limiting alcohol. Your clinician decides on medication based on your overall risk.

Limits to keep in mind

This tool sorts readings into categories; it cannot diagnose hypertension, which needs repeated readings over time. Wrist and finger devices are less accurate than validated upper-arm cuffs. If you are pregnant, different thresholds apply.

Questions people ask

Is 130/80 high blood pressure?

Yes. Since 2017, and confirmed in the 2025 guideline, 130/80 marks stage 1 hypertension in the U.S. guideline. Some other countries still use 140/90 as the threshold.

Why is my reading higher at the doctor's office?

Many people have 'white coat' effect. Home or 24-hour monitoring gives a truer picture.

What is a dangerous blood pressure?

Above 180/120 is the severe range. Re-check after 5 minutes of rest. If it stays that high, contact your doctor straight away; with symptoms such as chest pain or trouble speaking, call emergency services.
