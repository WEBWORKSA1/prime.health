How it works

Stride length is estimated from height: about 41.5% of height for men and 41.3% for women. Distance = steps × stride. Time comes from your chosen pace, and calories from the walking MET value for that pace:

kcal = MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes

Reading your result

A 2025 Lancet Public Health meta-analysis of 57 studies found that, compared with 2,000 steps a day, about 7,000 steps was linked to 47% lower risk of death from any cause, 25% lower cardiovascular disease and 38% lower dementia risk. Benefits kept rising toward 10,000 for some outcomes but flattened for others. Even moving from 2,000 to 4,000 steps helped.

Limits to keep in mind

Stride estimates can be 10% off; measure 10 strides on a track to personalise. Phone step counts undercount when the phone is in a bag.

Questions people ask

How many steps a day do I need?

About 7,000 captures most of the benefit for most adults. If you are below 4,000, add 1,000 a day each week.

How many steps is a mile?

About 2,000–2,500 for most adults, depending on height and pace.

Do steps count as exercise?

Brisk walking is moderate activity and counts toward the 150 minutes a week WHO recommends.
