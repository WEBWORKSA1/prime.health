Cholesterol calculator

Turn your lipid panel into the two numbers many experts watch most: non-HDL cholesterol and the total-to-HDL ratio.

How it works

Non-HDL cholesterol = total cholesterol − HDL   |   Ratio = total ÷ HDL

Non-HDL cholesterol captures every cholesterol-carrying particle that can lodge in artery walls (LDL, VLDL, remnants and Lp(a)). It does not need fasting and stays accurate when triglycerides are high, which is why the National Lipid Association treats it as a primary target. To convert mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply cholesterol values by 38.67 and triglycerides by 88.57.

HDL below 40 mg/dL in men or 50 mg/dL in women counts as low. LDL categories follow the long-standing NCEP bands (optimal below 100 mg/dL). Triglycerides below 150 mg/dL are normal. Your clinician combines these with age, blood pressure, diabetes and smoking to estimate 10-year risk.

Limits to keep in mind

Targets are lower if you already have heart disease, diabetes or very high risk, and are set with your clinician. LDL of 190 mg/dL (4.9 mmol/L) or more can point to familial hypercholesterolaemia, which runs in families and deserves early attention. Lipoprotein(a) is inherited and not shown on a standard panel; consider asking for it once.

Questions people ask

What is a good non-HDL cholesterol?

Below 130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) for most adults without heart disease.

Do I need to fast before a cholesterol test?

Usually not. Non-fasting panels are fine for most people; fasting may be requested if triglycerides are very high.

Can I raise HDL to cancel out high LDL?

No. Raising HDL with drugs did not cut heart attacks in trials. Lowering LDL and non-HDL is what reduces risk.
