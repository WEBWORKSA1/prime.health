How it works

Pregnancy is dated from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). Naegele's rule adds 280 days (40 weeks), adjusted here for cycles longer or shorter than 28 days:

Due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length − 28)

From a known conception date, the due date is 266 days later.

Reading your result

The milestone table shows when common checks typically happen: the anatomy scan around 18–22 weeks and glucose screening around 24–28 weeks. Full term is 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists notes that an ultrasound in the first trimester is the most accurate way to confirm the due date.

Limits to keep in mind

Only a small share of babies arrive on the due date itself. Irregular cycles, IVF and recent hormonal contraception change dating. Your care provider will set the official date.

Questions people ask

How accurate is a due date?

It is an estimate. Most babies are born within two weeks before or after it.

What if I don't remember my last period?

An early ultrasound can date the pregnancy accurately. Book an appointment with your provider.

How is IVF dated?

From the embryo transfer date and embryo age; ask your clinic for the exact date.
