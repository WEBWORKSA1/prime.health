How it works

We start at about 33 mL per kg of body weight, then add about 0.6 L per hour of exercise, 0.5 L for hot conditions, 0.3 L in pregnancy and 0.7 L when breastfeeding. For reference, the U.S. National Academies set adequate total water intakes of about 3.7 L a day for men and 2.7 L for women, roughly 80% of which comes from drinks.

Reading your result

Spread fluid through the day and drink to thirst. Urine that is pale yellow is a simple sign you are on track. Water, milk, tea and coffee all count; caffeinated drinks hydrate nearly as well as water at normal intakes.

Limits to keep in mind

Heart failure, kidney disease and some medicines require fluid limits; follow your clinician's instructions. Very high intakes over a short time can be dangerous, particularly during long endurance events.

Questions people ask

Do I really need 8 glasses a day?

It is a reasonable rough guide, not a rule. Needs vary with size, activity and climate.

Does coffee dehydrate you?

Not at usual intakes. Coffee and tea count toward your fluid total.

How do I know if I am dehydrated?

Dark urine, thirst, headache and fatigue are common signs.
