Waist-to-hip ratio calculator

Compares the narrowest point of your waist with the widest point of your hips. A higher ratio means more fat stored around the middle.

How it works

WHR = waist circumference ÷ hip circumference

The World Health Organization's 2008 expert report on waist measures links a ratio of 0.90 or more in men and 0.85 or more in women with substantially increased metabolic risk. The same report sets waist-circumference action levels: increased risk above 94 cm (37 in) in men and 80 cm (31.5 in) in women; substantially increased above 102 cm (40 in) and 88 cm (35 in).

Measure the waist midway between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone and the hips at the widest part of the buttocks, standing with feet together.

Reading your result

Below the cut-off for your sex is the target. Above it, central fat is likely contributing to risk even if your weight is normal. The second bar shows your waist alone against the WHO levels, which some clinicians prefer because it needs only one measurement.

Limits to keep in mind

The ratio can stay the same while both measures shrink, so also track waist on its own. Cut-offs differ for some populations; for many Asian adults, guidelines use lower waist thresholds (about 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women).

Questions people ask

What is a healthy waist-to-hip ratio?

Below 0.90 for men and below 0.85 for women, according to the World Health Organization.

Waist-to-hip or waist-to-height: which is better?

Both flag central fat. Waist-to-height has a single cut-off (0.5) for everyone and has slightly stronger evidence for predicting risk.

Why does my ratio go up as I get older?

After midlife, fat tends to shift toward the abdomen, especially after menopause, even when weight is stable.
