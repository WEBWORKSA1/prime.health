How it works

The planner applies the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommendations graded A or B, which means there is good evidence of net benefit, plus a few graded C that call for a personal decision. Examples:

  • Colorectal cancer screening from 45 to 75
  • Mammograms every two years from 40 to 74
  • Cervical screening from 21 to 65
  • Yearly low-dose CT lung screening from 50 to 80 for people with 20 or more pack-years who smoke or quit within 15 years
  • Diabetes screening from 35 to 70 with overweight or obesity
  • A one-time hepatitis C test from 18 to 79

Reading your result

Use the list as a conversation starter at your next check-up. “Recommended for you” means the evidence supports screening people with your profile; your clinician may adjust timing for family history, past results or symptoms. Screening is for people without symptoms; new symptoms need prompt assessment, not a screening test.

Limits to keep in mind

The planner follows U.S. guidance. Canada's Task Force on Preventive Health Care, the UK's NHS screening programmes and others differ in ages and intervals. It does not cover everything, such as vision, dental and hearing checks, or vaccines.

Questions people ask

What does a USPSTF grade A or B mean?

High or moderate certainty that the service has a net benefit. Under U.S. law, most private plans must cover A and B services without cost-sharing.

Why did breast screening start at 40?

In 2024 the USPSTF lowered the start age from 50 to 40 for screening every two years, citing rising incidence in women in their 40s.

I have a family history of cancer. Does this apply?

You may need earlier or more frequent screening. Share your family history with your clinician.
