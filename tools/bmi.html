BMI & BMI Prime calculator

Body mass index compares your weight with your height. BMI Prime divides it by 25, the top of the healthy range, so 1.00 is the line to stay at or under.

How it works

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres. In US units the same number comes from pounds and inches multiplied by 703.

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²   |   BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

BMI Prime, introduced in a 2006 medical paper, divides your BMI by 25, the upper limit of the healthy range:

BMI Prime = BMI ÷ 25

That turns BMI into a ratio. A BMI Prime of 1.00 sits exactly on the healthy limit. A value of 1.20 means you are 20% above it, and 0.90 means you have a 10% margin. It is the same information as BMI, expressed in a way that makes progress easy to read.

With Asian cut-offs, a BMI of 23–27.4 signals increased risk and 27.5 or more signals high risk, following the World Health Organization expert consultation. The calculator also shows the weight range that keeps you between BMI 18.5 and the healthy upper limit for your height.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Muscle looks like fat. Strength athletes often score as overweight while carrying little fat.
  • Fat location matters more than weight. Fat around the waist carries more risk. Check your waist-to-height ratio too.
  • Older adults can have a normal BMI with low muscle mass.
  • Not for children, teens or pregnancy. Under 20, BMI is read against age- and sex-specific percentiles.

Questions people ask

Is BMI accurate?

BMI is a fast, free screening number that tracks with health risk across large groups. It is less accurate for individuals with a lot of muscle, older adults and some ethnic groups, so pair it with waist measures.

What is a good BMI Prime?

Between 0.74 and 1.00, which equals a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9. Below 0.74 is underweight; above 1.00 is over the healthy limit.

Should I use the Asian cut-offs?

If you have South Asian, East Asian or Southeast Asian ancestry, the lower cut-offs better reflect the BMI at which risks of type 2 diabetes and heart disease begin to climb.
