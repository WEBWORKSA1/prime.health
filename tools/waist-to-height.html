Waist-to-height ratio calculator

One rule, two numbers: keep your waist to less than half your height. It flags belly fat that BMI misses.

Waist-to-height ratio

in

Halfway between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, after breathing out.

How it works

Divide your waist circumference by your height, using the same unit for both.

WHtR = waist ÷ height

Fat stored around the organs (visceral fat) drives insulin resistance, raised blood pressure and unhealthy cholesterol. Waist size tracks visceral fat far better than weight, and dividing by height makes the number fair for tall and short people. Research reviews have found waist-to-height ratio predicts heart and metabolic risk at least as well as BMI or waist alone, and the UK's NICE guidance adopted the 0.5 boundary for adults in 2022.

Reading your result

RatioMeaning
0.40–0.49Healthy central fat. No increased risk.
0.50–0.59Increased health risk. Worth acting on.
0.60 and aboveHigh health risk. Talk to a clinician about blood pressure, glucose and lipids.

A ratio below 0.40 can occur in very lean people; it is not itself a disease marker, but if you are also underweight by BMI, look at nutrition. The calculator shows the waist size that would put you exactly at 0.5.

Limits to keep in mind

The boundary values were set for adults. They are not designed for pregnancy, and some athletes with very large torsos may read high. Measure at the same point each time; moving the tape a few centimetres changes the result.

Questions people ask

Where exactly do I measure my waist?

Find the bottom of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone. Measure halfway between them, tape level, after a normal breath out. Do not suck in.

Is this better than BMI?

They answer different questions. BMI is about overall size; waist-to-height ratio is about where fat sits. Together they give a fuller picture.

How fast can it change?

Waist size responds to activity and diet within weeks. A 2–3 cm drop is meaningful.
