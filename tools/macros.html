How it works

Calories come from your TDEE (Mifflin-St Jeor × activity) plus the goal adjustment. Then:

  • Protein: 1.4–1.8 g per kg of body weight depending on style (at BMI over 30, a reference weight at BMI 27.5 is used).
  • Fat: 22–40% of calories depending on style.
  • Carbohydrate: the remaining calories (at least 50 g).

Energy values: protein and carbohydrate 4 kcal per gram, fat 9 kcal per gram.

Reading your result

All three styles fall within the National Academies' acceptable ranges (carbohydrate 45–65%, fat 20–35%, protein 10–35% of energy) or close to their edges for the lower-carb option. The best split is the one you can keep: hit your protein and calorie targets most days and let carbs and fat flex.

Limits to keep in mind

Targets are estimates. People with diabetes, kidney disease or on medicines that affect blood sugar should set macros with their care team.

Questions people ask

Do I need to hit my macros exactly?

No. Within 10% on most days is plenty. Protein and total calories matter most.

Is low-carb better for fat loss?

Trials show similar fat loss when calories and protein are matched. Choose what keeps you full and consistent.

How much fibre should I eat?

About 14 g per 1,000 kcal, roughly 25–38 g a day for most adults.
