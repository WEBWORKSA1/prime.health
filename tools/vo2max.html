VO₂ max calculator

VO₂ max is the most oxygen your body can use. It is one of the strongest predictors of how long and how well people live, and it responds to training at any age.

How it works

Resting heart-rate method (Uth–Sørensen, 2004):

VO₂ max ≈ 15.3 × HRmax ÷ HRrest

If you do not enter a measured maximum, the tool uses Tanaka's 208 − 0.7 × age.

Cooper 12-minute test (1968): warm up, then run or walk as far as you can in 12 minutes on a flat track.

VO₂ max ≈ (distance in metres − 504.9) ÷ 44.73

The result is shown in mL of oxygen per kg per minute and in METs (1 MET = 3.5 mL/kg/min).

Reading your result

Higher is better at every age. In a 2009 JAMA meta-analysis of 33 studies, each 1-MET increase in fitness was associated with about 13% lower risk of death from any cause and 15% lower risk of heart disease events. A 2018 study of more than 120,000 treadmill tests found people in the lowest fitness group had a higher risk of death than those with diabetes or a smoking history. Use the “next milestone” figure as your target for the next few months.

Limits to keep in mind

Field estimates usually fall within about 10% of a lab test but can miss by more. The resting method is weaker for very fit or unfit people and anyone on heart-rate-lowering medicine. Do not attempt a maximal run test if you have chest symptoms or heart disease without medical clearance.

Questions people ask

What is a good VO₂ max?

It depends on age and sex. Rather than chase a label, aim to sit above the average for your age and add 1 MET (3.5 mL/kg/min) over the next few months.

How do I raise my VO₂ max?

Combine plenty of easy aerobic work with one weekly session of hard intervals, such as 4 × 4 minutes at a hard effort with 3 minutes easy between.

Are smartwatch VO₂ max numbers reliable?

They are reasonable for tracking trends but can be off by 5–15% for individuals.
