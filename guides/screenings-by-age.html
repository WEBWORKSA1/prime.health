Preventive screenings by age: a plain-language checklist

Which health checks are recommended in your 20s, 30s, 40s, 50s, 60s and beyond, based on the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force, with notes on other countries.

Updated November 11, 20267 min read4 sources

Key takeaways

  • Blood pressure checks start at 18; cholesterol and heart-risk checks at 40.
  • Colorectal screening starts at 45; mammograms every two years from 40.
  • Lung CT screening applies from 50 to 80 for heavy current or recent smokers.
  • Screening is for people without symptoms; new symptoms need prompt assessment.

Screening tests look for disease before symptoms appear, when it is easier to treat. The trick is knowing which tests have strong evidence at which age. This checklist summarises U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommendations graded A or B for adults at average risk.

For all adults

  • Blood pressure: every 3–5 years from 18 to 39 if normal; every year from 40 or with risk factors.
  • Depression screening at routine visits; anxiety screening up to age 64.
  • Unhealthy alcohol use screening and brief counselling.
  • Tobacco: ask at every visit; support to quit for anyone who smokes.
  • HIV: at least once from 15 to 65.
  • Hepatitis C: once from 18 to 79.

20s and 30s

  • Cervical screening (women and people with a cervix): Pap test every 3 years from 21 to 29.
  • From 30, choose a Pap every 3 years, an HPV test every 5 years or both every 5 years.
  • Diabetes: from 35 if overweight or obese.

40s

  • Mammogram every 2 years from 40 to 74 (USPSTF lowered the start age from 50 in 2024).
  • Cholesterol and 10-year heart-risk check from 40 to guide statin decisions.
  • Colorectal cancer screening from 45: a stool test every 1–3 years depending on type, or colonoscopy every 10 years.

50s and 60s

  • Lung cancer: yearly low-dose CT from 50 to 80 for people with 20 or more pack-years who smoke or quit within the past 15 years.
  • Prostate (PSA): from 55 to 69, an individual decision after discussing benefits and harms.
  • Bone density: before 65 for postmenopausal women with risk factors.

65 and older

  • Bone density for all women from 65.
  • Abdominal aortic aneurysm: one ultrasound for men 65–75 who have ever smoked.
  • Falls prevention: exercise programs that build strength and balance.
  • Colorectal screening from 76 to 85 is selective, based on health and past results.

If you are at higher risk

A strong family history of cancer, heart disease or diabetes, certain genetic findings, or past abnormal results can mean starting earlier or testing more often. Bring your family history to your next appointment.

Vaccines

Vaccines are prevention too. Ask which are due for your age and health, and check your national immunization schedule.

Make it personal

Use the screening planner to generate your own list from your age, sex and smoking history, then book what is due.

Questions people ask

Who is the USPSTF?

The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force is an independent panel of experts in prevention and primary care that grades the evidence for screening tests and preventive services.

Are these the same in Canada or the UK?

The principles are similar but ages and intervals differ. For example, the UK's national bowel screening programme starts at 50 in England, and Canada's Task Force sets its own recommendations. Follow your local program.

Will insurance cover these tests?

In the U.S., most private plans must cover USPSTF A and B services without cost-sharing. Coverage elsewhere follows national programs.
