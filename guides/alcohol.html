Alcohol and health: what changed in the evidence

Health authorities have moved from 'a drink a day is good for you' to 'less is better'. Here is what changed, what it means for your numbers and how to cut back.

Updated November 11, 20266 min read4 sources

Key takeaways

  • WHO states there is no safe level of alcohol for cancer risk.
  • Canada's 2023 guidance puts low risk at 2 or fewer standard drinks a week.
  • A 2025 U.S. Surgeon General advisory called alcohol a leading preventable cause of cancer.
  • Cutting back improves sleep, blood pressure and triglycerides within weeks.

For decades, a glass of red wine was presented as heart medicine. The evidence has shifted, and major health bodies have changed their advice.

What changed

Early studies that found lower heart risk among moderate drinkers often compared them with “non-drinkers” who included people who had quit because of illness. Better-designed analyses, including genetic studies, find little or no protective effect. Meanwhile the evidence linking alcohol to at least seven cancers, including breast, bowel, liver, mouth and throat, has strengthened.

What authorities now say

  • World Health Organization (2023): when it comes to cancer risk, no level of drinking is safe.
  • Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health (2023): 2 or fewer standard drinks a week is low risk; 3–6 is moderate risk; 7 or more is increasingly high risk.
  • U.S. Surgeon General (January 2025): alcohol is the third leading preventable cause of cancer in the U.S., after tobacco and obesity, and labels should carry a cancer warning.

What alcohol does to your Prime Numbers

  • Blood pressure: regular drinking raises it; cutting back lowers systolic pressure by about 4 mm Hg on average in people who drink more than two drinks a day.
  • Sleep: it fragments the second half of the night and suppresses REM sleep.
  • Triglycerides and waist: alcohol adds energy and visceral fat and raises triglycerides.
  • Heart rhythm: even moderate drinking is linked to higher risk of atrial fibrillation.

Practical ways to cut back

  1. Count standard drinks for a week without changing anything.
  2. Pick alcohol-free days, at least three a week.
  3. Alternate each drink with water or a zero-alcohol option.
  4. Use smaller glasses and lower-strength drinks.
  5. Plan social time around food, walks or activities rather than bars.

When to get help

If you find it hard to cut down, drink to cope with stress or have morning cravings, talk to a clinician. Stopping suddenly after heavy daily drinking can be dangerous; medical support makes it safe.

Questions people ask

Isn't red wine good for the heart?

Older studies suggesting benefit had design flaws, such as counting former heavy drinkers among non-drinkers. Newer analyses find little or no protective effect.

What is a standard drink?

In the U.S., 14 g of pure alcohol: about 355 mL (12 oz) of 5% beer, 150 mL (5 oz) of 12% wine or 44 mL (1.5 oz) of 40% spirits. Canada uses 13.45 g; a UK unit is 8 g.

Where can I get help cutting down?

Talk to your clinician. Many countries offer free helplines and online programs, and medicines can help people with alcohol use disorder.
