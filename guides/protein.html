How much protein do you need? Evidence by age and goal

The RDA prevents deficiency; it is not the target for building or keeping muscle. Here is how much protein you need by age and goal, how to spread it and where to get it.

Updated November 11, 20267 min read5 sources

Key takeaways

  • The RDA is 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day: a floor, not an optimum.
  • Adults over 65 benefit from 1.0–1.2 g/kg; active people from 1.2–2.0 g/kg.
  • Aim for 25–40 g at each of three or four meals.
  • Healthy kidneys handle higher intakes; kidney disease needs medical advice.

Protein supplies the amino acids your body uses to repair muscle, make enzymes and hormones, and support immunity. Most adults eat enough to avoid deficiency. Fewer eat enough, at the right times, to build and keep muscle as they age.

The RDA is a floor

The Recommended Dietary Allowance of 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day was set to prevent deficiency in nearly all healthy adults. For a 75 kg person that is 60 g. It was not designed to maximise strength, recovery or healthy ageing.

The older-adult range comes from the PROT-AGE expert group; exercise ranges from the International Society of Sports Nutrition. If you carry a lot of excess weight, base the calculation on a goal or adjusted weight rather than current weight.

Spread it across the day

Muscle building responds to each protein-rich meal. Research suggests about 0.4 g per kg per meal, roughly 25–40 g for most adults, across three or four meals. Many people eat 10 g at breakfast and 60 g at dinner; shifting some to breakfast is one of the easiest upgrades.

Plant-based eaters

Combine legumes, soy, whole grains, nuts and seeds through the day and aim toward the upper end of your range. Soy, pea and mixed plant protein powders are useful top-ups.

When to be careful

People with chronic kidney disease may need to limit protein and should follow their nephrologist or dietitian. Pregnancy and breastfeeding raise needs. Protein shakes are not a replacement for meals in children or teens.

Questions people ask

Can I eat too much protein?

Intakes up to about 2 g per kg a day are well tolerated by healthy adults. Beyond that there is little extra benefit and less room for fibre-rich foods.

Are protein powders necessary?

No. They are a convenient top-up. Whole foods also bring fibre, vitamins and minerals.

Is plant protein as good as animal protein?

Plant proteins are slightly less digestible and lower in some amino acids, so aim a little higher and combine sources such as soy, legumes and grains.
